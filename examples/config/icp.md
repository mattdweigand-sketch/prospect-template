# Fictional example ICP

This fictional AI research example illustrates the workflow. Setup replaces its business choices; it is not approved live configuration.

**Target organizations with 200–5,000 employees whose teams repeatedly turn research and company information into decisions, client work, or operating deliverables.**

Use this to choose accounts, initiative owners, and functional sponsors.

## Company parameters

| Parameter | Target |
|---|---|
| Company size | **200–5,000 employees**, inclusive. This is the seller's territory, not an Example Research product requirement. |
| Account scope | The actual buying entity. Verify dated headcount and parent/subsidiary relationships. A fund manager's headcount excludes portfolio-company employees. |
| Workforce | Knowledge-intensive teams: research, consulting, professional services, commercial functions, or operations. Total employees do not equal potential users. |
| Recurring work | Research, analysis, client materials, proposals, reporting, monitoring, or assembling information across systems. Identify one plausible team workflow; confirm source access and review during discovery. |
| Other filters | No additional revenue, funding-stage, ownership, or geographic filters. Verify assigned territory and buying requirements. |

## Target personas

For company-wide programs, find the AI, transformation, enablement, or IT owner. For departmental initiatives, find the functional sponsor and implementation lead.

An executive's post can identify a sponsor; verify who owns delivery. Use the titles below as search terms.

### Initiative owners

| Persona | Example titles | Responsibility to look for |
|---|---|---|
| **AI and transformation owner** | Chief AI Officer, Head of AI, Head of Innovation, Chief Transformation Officer | Selecting AI priorities, sponsoring evaluations, and extending adoption across teams |
| **AI program and enablement owner** | AI Program Manager, AI Enablement Lead, Head of Automation, AI Adoption Lead | Delivering pilots, introducing team workflows, training employees, and measuring adoption |
| **IT and data owner** | CIO, CTO, IT Director, Enterprise Architect, Head of Data | Evaluating tools, connecting company information, and supporting internal AI systems |

### Functional sponsors

Find the leader whose team would use the workflow, including where no central AI team exists. Confirm the proposed use case.

| Persona | Example titles | Work to explore |
|---|---|---|
| **Consulting and advisory leader** | Managing Partner, Practice Head, Engagement Manager, Principal | Client research, market studies, proposals, presentations |
| **Investment and research leader** | Head of Research, Investment Partner, Portfolio Manager, Operating Partner | Company research, diligence, investment memos, portfolio reports |
| **Investor and client-service leader** | Head of Investor Relations, Wealth Adviser, Head of Client Service | Investor updates, client briefs, market updates |
| **Legal and knowledge leader** | Practice Partner, General Counsel, Knowledge Director, Head of Legal Operations | Legal research, document comparison, regulatory monitoring |
| **Strategy and executive operations** | CEO, COO, Chief of Staff, Head of Strategy, Corporate Development Director | Decision briefs, operating reviews, market maps, acquisition research |
| **Sales and revenue leader** | CRO, VP of Sales, Head of Revenue Operations, Head of Enablement | Account research, deal preparation, proposals, shared research processes |
| **Marketing and product leader** | CMO, Head of Product Marketing, Product Director | Competitive research, campaign briefs, launch materials, customer-feedback synthesis |
| **Finance and operations leader** | CFO, FP&A Director, Controller, Head of Operations, Procurement Director | Management reports, variance analysis, supplier comparisons, reconciliation |
| **Scientific and policy leader** | Research Director, Medical Affairs Director, Regulatory Affairs Director, Policy Director | Literature reviews, evidence tables, policy and research briefs |

Practitioners and team leads can champion the first workflow. Involve security, privacy, compliance, procurement, and legal when their remit is relevant. Verify responsibility and authority; seniority alone is insufficient.

## Pursuit boundaries

Prioritize workflow fit, current AI activity, and an identifiable owner. Accounts can fit before a public initiative is visible or budget is confirmed. Keep unknowns explicit; signals establish activity and timing separately.

Route accounts outside the employee range or seeking only coding assistance elsewhere. Hold pursuit when essential data is inaccessible, mandatory requirements are unmet, or the buyer requires unreviewed consequential output. Deprioritize accounts without a recurring team need. Research embedded in a customer's product is a separate API opportunity.
